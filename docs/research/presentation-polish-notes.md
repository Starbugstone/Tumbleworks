# Presentation polish: belts, visual hierarchy and waiting

Research date: **31 October 2026**.

Method: Read §3, “Feel and presentation,” of the existing mechanics research first. Then fetched the official Google Play listings and inspected their image assets for Marble Sort!, Garawell/Voodoo’s Loop Sort, Pixel Flow!, Screw Jam and Goods Sort; sampled both embedded Marble Sort! gameplay clips from Baijing, including consecutive belt close-ups; inspected two Loop Sort actual-play screenshot collages from Sohu; and checked Rollic’s support pages plus Ninja Kiwi’s official fast-forward FAQ and a Bloons TD 6 store screenshot. Pixel Flow!’s fetched listing displayed **#8 top-grossing puzzle**; current grossing ranks for the other comparators were not established. Store artwork is identified separately from gameplay. **Confirmed** means directly observed or explicitly stated on a fetched official page; **Implied** means an inference; **Guessed** means unverified or proposed. Visual proportions are approximate screen-space estimates, not engine specifications. No app was played and no phone performance was measured.

## A. How movement is shown

- **Confirmed — Marble Sort!, gameplay:** The charcoal-grey belt contains a repeating chain of rounded, recessed cells. Consecutive close-ups around 8 seconds in the Level 1 clip show those cells changing position around the oval even when it is nearly empty. They are **on the carrying surface**, not beside it. They read as shallow sockets, not transverse white ladder rungs. Cargo and this moving pattern provide separate motion cues. [M1]
- **Confirmed — Marble Sort!, visual estimates:** Each cell is approximately **0.8–1 marble diameter** wide, with a pitch around **1–1.3 diameters**. Its thin shaded edge is only a small fraction of a marble diameter, roughly **0.04–0.08**. Cell-to-belt contrast is subdued grey-on-grey; the stationary white outer rim and central divider are much brighter. The store close-up corroborates the cells and rim. Exact luminance contrast was not measured. [MS]
- **Confirmed — Loop Sort, actual-play frames:** The figure-eight and rectangular loop have a largely plain, dark blue-purple channel bounded by narrow purple rims. No dense crossbars, repeated bright arrows, exposed rollers or hazard lights are visible. Many small cubes occupy several positions across the width; their stream traces the route. Rims are much thinner than the channel, approximately a fraction of one travelling cube’s width in these images. [LA]
- **Confirmed — Loop Sort, Level 7 store image:** Very faint, darker chevrons appear on the open right-hand carrying surface, roughly half a tiny cube wide and several cube widths apart. They are not bright cross-channel bars. **Implied:** cargo remains the stronger cue. A still cannot establish whether these marks scroll. The giant white circular arrow in the Level 48 image is promotional instruction artwork, not evidence of a rotating rail or speed button. [L7, LS]
- **Confirmed — Pixel Flow!, store gameplay compositions:** The pigs sit on a broad, smooth blue/purple rectangular track with a thin lighter edge. No repeated transverse bars or dash texture is visible; firing pigs and projectiles provide activity. One different promotional render includes a ribbed entrance roller, but it does not establish animated rollers throughout gameplay. **Implied:** cargo movement can explain an otherwise visually quiet track. [P2, P3]
- **Confirmed — Screw Jam / Goods Sort:** Their inspected images show stationary boards/toolboxes and shelves, respectively, rather than belts. They offer useful clutter comparisons, but no evidence about conveyor texture animation. [SJ, G2, G3]

## B. Backgrounds, framing and decoration

- **Confirmed — Marble Sort!, gameplay:** A plain muted periwinkle field surrounds a pale blue-grey hopper and white receiver bed. There is no floor grid, machinery vignette, numbered dock signage or industrial perimeter furniture. The machine spans roughly **90% of screen width** and **70% of portrait height**, below the HUD. Early levels leave substantial **empty space inside the hopper**, rather than filling it with decoration. [M2]
- **Confirmed — Loop Sort, actual play:** The background is an almost uniform dark indigo field with a slight tonal variation. Trucks and track form the composition; there are no surrounding buildings or workshop props. The toy nearly reaches both side margins and occupies roughly the middle **65–75% of height**. Small HUD controls float above and below it. The figure-eight’s empty lobes remain empty. [LA, LO]
- **Confirmed — camera appearance:** Loop Sort shows mostly truck roofs and cargo tops, with short visible vehicle sides: a high, near-overhead view. Marble Sort!’s hopper is almost diagrammatic/front-readable while rims and boxes retain shallow depth. **Guessed:** exact projection type and camera tilt cannot be recovered reliably from these captures. [LO, M2]
- **Confirmed — comparators:** Screw Jam’s artwork uses an off-white, almost blank field, with layered glass shapes providing depth. Goods Sort places packed wooden shelving against dark blue-grey empty surroundings; another image uses an isolated freestanding shelf. Pixel Flow!’s track encloses the pixel picture against a soft purple field. These examples concentrate detail **in the puzzle**, with little decorative scenery outside its silhouette. [SJ, G2, G3, P2, P3]
- **Implied — composition lesson:** Dense playable contents and a calm setting coexist. Goods Sort’s busy product labels are targets the player must inspect, unlike decorative studs or stencilled branding. Store captions and mascots occupy advertising space and should not be counted as persistent play HUD. [G2, LS, P3]

## C. Speed-up and fast-forward controls

- **Confirmed — bounded observation:** Neither Marble Sort! gameplay clip shows a labelled speed control, double-chevron control, or multiplier during play. Loop Sort’s inspected actual-play frames show level/capacity/settings and three bottom controls, with no identifiable speed label. **Guessed / unresolved:** this does not prove such a feature is absent from every version, later level or settings menu. [M1, M2, LA]
- **Confirmed — explicit control elsewhere:** Ninja Kiwi’s developer-posted Bloons TD 6 FAQ identifies Space as “Start round/toggle fast forward.” Its official store screenshot shows a **green double-triangle button at bottom right**, separate from the main field. This is evidence for a **toggle**, not press-and-hold. The official FAQ does not state a numerical multiplier; the inspected screenshot displays arrows rather than “2×” or “3×.” [BF, BI]
- **Implied — active-state cue:** Changing a play symbol to double triangles can communicate speed without a large banner. An on/off comparison was not obtained, so the screenshot alone does not establish the button’s inactive colour or an activation animation. [BI]
- **Confirmed — Screw Jam:** Rollic describes tap-activated bottom boosters: Extra Slot, Hammer and Double Toolbox. These alter the puzzle; they are not documented time multipliers. Do not interpret “speed up unscrewing” marketing as proof of fast-forward. [SH]
- **Guessed — Tumbleworks choice:** A hold control is a suitable response to this player’s waiting complaint, but **no hold-to-fast-forward precedent was verified in the sampled sorters**. Suggested multiplier, placement and active indicator below are design proposals, not copied reference behaviour.

## D. HUD and capacity information

- **Confirmed — Loop Sort:** Actual-play captures put a small level pill at top left, a **cube icon plus fraction** near top centre, and a gear at top right. Three compact round/squarish icon buttons sit at bottom centre. No dock numbers or explanatory paragraphs are painted around the trucks. Top controls are only a few percent of screen height; the whole bottom button group occupies approximately half the width. [LO]
- **Implied — fraction meaning:** The fraction is consistent with occupancy/capacity, but screenshots do not prove its unit accounting. Its useful presentation pattern is **one icon + one fraction**, not a sentence. Keep Tumbleworks’ authoritative logical units rather than equating tiny rendered marbles with counted capacity. [LO]
- **Confirmed — Marble Sort!, early gameplay:** Settings, level and coin balance occupy the top strip; three locked future tools form a second small row. No separate belt-capacity number is visible. Three circular recesses on each receiving box show its local demand directly. This build is visually quiet around the machine but still has more progression HUD than Tumbleworks needs. [M2]
- **Confirmed — Screw Jam:** Promotional images expose three-hole toolboxes and five spare circular slots instead of a textual storage meter. Rollic’s official support confirms five temporary slots and bottom booster icons. **Implied:** capacity can live in the toy when each place remains clearly countable. [SJ, SH, SS]
- **Guessed / unresolved — Goods Sort and Pixel Flow!:** Inspected store compositions omit or crop much of the normal HUD. They cannot establish exact in-play timer, settings or booster placement. Pixel Flow!’s pigs visibly carry ammunition numbers; those are per-actor values, not a global conveyor-capacity meter. [P3, G2]

## E. Colour, materials and lighting

- **Confirmed — Marble Sort!:** Marbles have saturated colour and small bright highlights. Recessed cells and tray walls use shallow shading; soft contact shadows separate marbles from their supports. The surroundings are considerably less saturated than the cargo. There is a bright stationary belt rim, but no visible cyan emissive perimeter or bloom halo around the machine. [MS]
- **Confirmed — Loop Sort:** Purple vehicle shells and rails have lighter top edges and darker sides. Cargo blocks are strongly saturated and visually distinct from the dark ground. Actual-play frames do not show perimeter light bulbs or atmospheric glow. The store artwork adds conspicuous glow to tutorial/booster graphics, so it is weaker evidence for gameplay lighting. [LO, LS, L7]
- **Confirmed — comparators:** Screw Jam uses pale translucent plates with fine white edges and darker screw crosses; Goods Sort uses darker shelf cavities and small contact shadows behind colourful objects. Pixel Flow! uses bevel highlights and underside shadows to separate tiny blocks; some promotional renders have much stronger purple glow than the gameplay-shaped compositions. [SJ, G2, P2, P3]
- **Implied — accessibility limit:** Saturation and gloss help objects read as toys but do not establish colour-blind accessibility. These references do not justify removing Tumbleworks’ permanent container patterns or narrowing the yellow/teal luminance gap.

## Recommendations for Tumbleworks

All numerical targets below are **Guessed starting values for visual tuning**, not measurements of proprietary games.

- Remove the grid floor, amber hazard studs, machinery props, platform game-name stencil and numbered dock labels. Keep only surfaces needed to understand the path, pile and receivers.
- Use one muted blue-grey or indigo background with an optional broad gradient under **5 percentage points of relative luminance**; omit patterned floors and peripheral animation.
- Replace the **36–48 bright tick-bars** with a scrolling chain of shallow rounded cells: approximately **0.8 marble diameter** across, **1.2 diameters** apart, with edges **0.04 diameters** thick. Start around **8–12 percentage points of luminance difference** from the belt and inspect on a phone. Keep logical capacity slots intact.
- Desaturate the belt and replace cyan neon rails with narrow matte rims. Let cargo provide the strongest colour/contrast; preserve a readable stationary boundary at bends and the figure-eight bridge.
- Frame playable bounds to approximately **90–94% of usable phone width**. Exclude decorative bases from camera fitting; reserve room for truck departures and bottom controls.
- Preserve one slim top row: level pill, compact **belt/cube icon + `6/15`**, sound. Put title, difficulty, pattern legend and detailed explanations behind the level pill. Make a full-capacity state readable without flashing the perimeter.
- Add a **44–48 CSS-pixel hold button**, `≫`, in the lower safe margin near the existing controls, clear of receivers. Show `2×` inside it while pressed; use a steady filled active state. Teach “hold” once, then keep the control icon-led.
- Start fast-forward on pointer/keyboard down; return immediately to `1×` on release, cancellation, lost capture, focus loss, page hiding or level transition. Keep undo/restart semantics intact; add no latching toggle to the requested hold interaction.
- Start with **2×**; consider **3×** only after testing. Advance the complete gameplay clock consistently—including pile admission, transport, intake and queue advancement—using existing safe simulation steps. A full lap must preserve encounters and accounting; movement is not a teleport.
- Concentrate feedback at the accepted target: source opening within **100 ms**, brief local receiver lift/sparkle on completion, then queue advance. Keep the next legal action available during animation; avoid idle bouncing, chasing lights and screen-wide flashes.
- Keep plain glossy marbles and put **ring/diamond/triangle/star** patterns on visible container surfaces and cabs. Use soft contact shadows and modest bevel highlights; reserve high contrast for playable objects and essential state.
- Review the result in narrow/desktop views and grayscale, with reduced motion and rapid input. Check empty-belt movement, full-loop waiting, press cancellation and fast-forward release; make sure neither low-contrast cells nor the hold button obscure capacity or targets.
